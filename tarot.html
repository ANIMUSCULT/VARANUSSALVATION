Your readings

    How to read

    The craft, in the order readers use it

    Look first

    Before any meaning, look at the picture: who is in it, where they face, what they hold, the weather. Say what you see out loud. The meaning grows out of the picture, not the other way round.

    The place it lands

    Every spread gives each place a job: the past, the challenge, the outcome. Read the card through its place. A gentle card in the place of the challenge is a challenge that comes softly.

    Reversed cards

    A card upside down is the same force blocked, turned inward, arriving late or in excess. Some readers leave reversals out and let the neighbours decide; switch them off in the bar if you read that way.

    Neighbours

    Each suit belongs to an element: Wands fire, Cups water, Swords air, Pentacles earth; every trump has one too. Fire and air feed each other, so do water and earth. Fire and water fight, so do air and earth. A card beside friends is strong; beside enemies it weakens. A card between two cards that fight each other stands on its own.

    The whole table

    Count before you read. Many trumps: forces bigger than the asker are moving. A suit in the majority tells you what the matter is really about; a missing suit is a message too. Three or four of one rank change the reading. Add every number on the table and reduce it to a trump: the quintessence, the hidden lesson.

    The seal

    This desk keeps an old rule of augury: the question is declared before the signs are watched. Your question, the spread and the minute are sealed first, and the cards follow from the seal. Same question, same minute, same cards. Ask better, not more.

    What the cards won’t do

    He doesn’t read bodies: no questions about illness, pregnancy or anyone’s death. Ask about the rest of your life.
